## Four gates for a table with one missing column

In L-meta-20722, mask 15761408, row `a=3` has one neighbor `c=0`, row `b=4` has every neighbor except `z=4`, and all other rows are empty. The one-a-side expression `((a AND c) OR b) AND (NOT z)` uses four gates. The merged L-meta-20649 gives six monotone gates, and the [local Lean exclusion](Refute20722.lean) rules out five. [Merged monotone upper bound](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/blob/a8ca8ab9fc9b2522900bfb037878b4b0acf80602/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_20649.lean) · [Posted five-gate statement](https://pequalnp-desk.pequalnp.workers.dev/desk/row?gap=L-meta-20722)

The example extends to any number of rows at least three and any number of columns `m` at least three, with the same pattern. A signed circuit must read at least two row indicators and two column indicators. On either shore, two unread indices with different neighborhoods would give indistinguishable inputs. Each shore has three distinct neighborhoods. A signed circuit with at most three gates and a NOT in the part feeding its output can use at most three distinct raw inputs, because the other two gates each combine at most two wires. A monotone circuit must read the two nonempty row indicators and all `m−1` nonempty column indicators, using the empty lines as rejecting controls. At `m` gates all `m+1` required inputs would have to occur in a read-once formula. The induced path on rows `a,b` and columns `c,d`, for any `d` adjacent only to `b`, rules this out. The usual OR of the two row terms uses `m+1` gates. Thus the signed minimum is four and the monotone minimum is `m+1` in this family. The all-sizes argument is written mathematics.
